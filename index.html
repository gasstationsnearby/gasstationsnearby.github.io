Running low? Here's how this helps

GasStationNearby answers one question fast: where is the closest place to fuel up right now? Instead of scrolling through ads and sponsored pins, you get a clean list of real stations from the OpenStreetMap database — the same community-built map used by apps and researchers worldwide — sorted purely by how far each station is from you.

Every result shows the station name or brand, its address where OpenStreetMap has one, the straight-line distance in miles, opening hours where recorded, and a directions button that hands you straight to Google Maps for the actual road route. If you're planning ahead, search any city, town or ZIP code and pick a radius of 2, 5, 10 or 25 miles.

How It Works

GasStationNearby is a small tool with a simple job: show you the real gas stations closest to where you are (or where you're heading), as quickly as possible. Here's exactly what happens when you use it — no black boxes.

1

You share a location

Tap “Find Gas Stations Near Me” and your browser asks permission to share your approximate position. Or skip that entirely and type a city, town or ZIP code into the search box.

2

We ask OpenStreetMap

The page queries the OpenStreetMap Overpass API for every fuel station mapped within your chosen radius, and the Nominatim geocoder turns place names into coordinates when you search by text.

3

You get a sorted list

Distances are calculated with the haversine formula and the stations are listed nearest first, each with its address, opening hours where recorded, and a directions button that opens the route in Google Maps.

Where the data comes from

All station data comes from OpenStreetMap, the free, community-built map of the world. Volunteers survey and record gas stations — their locations, brand names, addresses, opening hours and fuel types — and that data is openly licensed under the Open Database License (ODbL). We query it live each time you search, so you're seeing the current state of the map, not a stale copy stored by us.

What we deliberately don't show

Fuel prices. There is no free, public, worldwide feed of live pump prices, and prices change too often for static data to stay honest. Rather than invent numbers, we leave prices out entirely and focus on what we can show truthfully: where stations are, how far away they are, and when they're open. Our cheapest gas guide covers practical ways to pay less.

Ratings and reviews. OpenStreetMap doesn't collect them, so we don't display any. A station's position in the list is decided by distance alone — never by advertising.

Your privacy, briefly

Your location is handled entirely inside your browser. It is used to ask OpenStreetMap's public services for nearby stations and is never sent to, stored by, or logged by this website — this site has no server and no database. The full details are in our Privacy Policy.

If something looks wrong

Because the map is community-maintained, a brand-new station might be missing or an entry might lack an address. Anyone can fix that by editing OpenStreetMap — corrections usually appear in our results within days. If the tool itself misbehaves, let us know.

How to Find the Cheapest Gas Near You

Fuel is one of those costs that quietly eats a budget — a few cents per gallon difference adds up to real money over a year of fill-ups. We can't show you live prices (no free public price feed exists, and we won't invent numbers), but the good news is that finding cheaper gas is mostly about habits, not luck. Here are the strategies that genuinely work.

Compare stations a few streets apart

Prices often differ noticeably between stations only minutes apart — a station on a busy highway junction or right off an exit usually charges more than one a couple of streets into town. Use the finder above with a 5 or 10 mile radius to see your options, then check the prices on the signs as you pass. The cheapest station is frequently not the closest one.

Fill up at warehouse clubs and supermarkets

Warehouse clubs and large supermarket fuel stations are consistently among the cheapest places to fill up, because fuel is used to draw in shoppers. If you already hold a membership or shop somewhere with a fuel station attached, it's usually worth making it your default stop.

Use loyalty and rewards programmes

Many fuel brands and grocery chains run points schemes that take a few cents off per gallon. The savings per fill-up are small, but they're automatic once you're enrolled, and they stack up over a year of driving.

Time your fill-ups

In many areas, prices tend to rise before weekends and holidays, when demand peaks, and ease off early in the week. Topping up on a quiet weekday morning instead of Friday evening can save a little almost every time — and the queues are shorter, too.

Don't drive out of your way for a small saving

A cheaper station only saves money if the detour is short. Driving several extra miles to save a couple of cents per gallon can burn more fuel than the saving is worth. As a rule of thumb, a small detour for a big price gap is worth it; a long detour for a tiny gap is not.

Use the right fuel grade

Check your owner's manual. Most cars designed for regular fuel gain nothing from premium — you're paying extra for octane the engine can't use. Only buy the higher grade if the manufacturer actually requires or recommends it.

Drive gently and keep tyres inflated

The cheapest gallon is the one you never burn. Smooth acceleration, steady speeds, an empty roof rack, and tyres kept at the recommended pressure all cut consumption — under-inflated tyres alone can waste a few percent of your fuel.

Plan stops on long trips

Motorway and highway service stations are famously the most expensive places to fuel. Before a long drive, use the search box above to find stations just off your route in the towns you'll pass — filling up there instead of at a service plaza can make a big difference on a full tank.

A quick word on price apps

Crowdsourced price-reporting apps and your national or state motoring organisation can show recent reported prices in some countries. They're a useful cross-check, but treat any single reported price with care — it may be hours or days old, and prices change daily. The price on the station's sign when you arrive is the only one that counts.

The bottom line

Know your options, don't pay highway prices by default, join the free rewards schemes, and burn less fuel overall. Combine those habits with the station finder to choose where to stop, and you'll reliably pay less than the driver who always uses the first station they see.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers about how the finder works, where the data comes from, and what the tool can and can't do.

How does GasStationNearby find gas stations near me?

When you tap the “Find Gas Stations Near Me” button, your browser shares your approximate location with the page. The tool then asks the OpenStreetMap Overpass API for every fuel station recorded around that point, calculates the straight-line distance to each one, and lists them closest first. Your location is used only in your browser and is never stored or sent to this website.

Does GasStationNearby show live gas prices?

No. There is no free, public, worldwide feed of live fuel prices, so we do not show prices at all rather than guess or invent them. We show station locations, distances, addresses and opening hours from OpenStreetMap. For prices, check the station's own signage or a dedicated price-reporting service in your country.

Is GasStationNearby free to use?

Yes. The tool is completely free, needs no account and no sign-up, and there are no limits on how many searches you can run. It is built on free public data from OpenStreetMap contributors.

Why is a station missing or listed with incomplete details?

Station data comes from OpenStreetMap, a map built by volunteers. Most stations are recorded, but a brand-new station may not have been added yet, and some entries lack an address or opening hours. Anyone can improve the map by editing OpenStreetMap directly — fixes usually show up here within days.

Can I search for gas stations in another city or ZIP code?

Yes. Type any city, town or ZIP/postcode into the search box instead of using your location — handy for planning a trip or checking an area you're moving to. The place is looked up with the free Nominatim geocoder and the same station search runs around that point.

Is my location data stored or shared?

No. Your coordinates are used in your browser only, to query OpenStreetMap services for nearby stations. This website has no server and no database, so nothing about your location is stored, logged or sold. See the Privacy Policy for the full picture.

How accurate are the distances shown?

Distances are straight-line distances calculated with the haversine formula between your location and each station. The actual driving distance will usually be a little longer because roads are not straight. Tap “Get Directions” on any result to see the real road route in Google Maps.

What do I do if no stations are found?

Try a larger search radius — 10 or 25 miles — especially in rural areas where stations are spread out. If you searched by place name, check the spelling or try the nearest larger town. You can also widen the radius and search again in one tap.

Does the tool work outside the United States?

Yes. OpenStreetMap covers most of the world, so the finder works wherever contributors have mapped fuel stations — in many countries you'll see them labelled as petrol stations, but the search is the same. Distances are shown in miles, and coverage detail varies by country.

How do I get directions to a station?

Every result has a “Get Directions” button. It opens Google Maps with a route from your current position to that station, where you can start turn-by-turn navigation in the Maps app or your browser.

About GasStationNearby

GasStationNearby exists for one small, urgent moment: the fuel light comes on, you're somewhere unfamiliar, and you need to know where the nearest station is — now, not after scrolling through ads, pop-ups and sponsored listings.

The tool is built entirely on free, open data. Station locations come from OpenStreetMap, the community-built map of the world maintained by hundreds of thousands of volunteers, queried live through the Overpass API. Place searches use the Nominatim geocoder. Because the data is open, we can offer the finder completely free, with no account and no tracking of your location.

What we stand for

  • Honesty about data. We show what the data actually contains — locations, distances, addresses, opening hours. We never invent fuel prices, ratings or reviews to make a listing look richer than it is.
  • Privacy by design. Your location never leaves your browser except to query OpenStreetMap's public services. There is no account, no database, and nothing to leak.
  • Speed over clutter. One button, one list, sorted by distance. That's the whole product, and we intend to keep it that way.

What the tool doesn't do

We don't sell fuel, take bookings, or process payments. We don't show live prices, because no free public price feed exists — our cheapest gas guide explains the habits that actually lower what you pay at the pump. And because OpenStreetMap is volunteer-built, a station may occasionally be missing or short on detail; if you spot an error, editing the map fixes it for everyone.
